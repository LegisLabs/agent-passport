The shift

Agents are already buying. The question is no longer whether they will pay, but on whose authority.

Source: Adyen Retail Report 2026, UK: use of AI assistants when shopping rose from 12% to 28% in a year. 2027 is a projection at the same pace, not a survey.

59% of UK consumers have used an AI agent when shopping (Ingrid, 2026) · 44% would let AI complete the purchase (Adyen, 2026) · 6 in 10 would drop an agent after one mistake (ACI Worldwide, 2026).

The numbers

Payment fraud is already an industry. £1.28 billion was taken from UK customers last year, and every pound of it was authorised by someone who believed what they were shown.

Source: UK Finance, Annual Fraud Report 2026. Losses reported to UK Finance.

£576m lost to authorised push payment fraud in 2025, up 19% (UK Finance) · 4 of 26 AI models tricked by hidden text into paying (SecurityWeek) · HM Treasury, July 2026: a Know Your Agent standard, high priority (GOV.UK).

The problem

An agent believes what it is shown too. Give it the right document and it will pay the wrong account with perfect confidence, at machine speed, without a second look. As agents take over the invoice desk, that industry gains a workforce that never sleeps.

The solution

The only defence that scales with it is a mandate the agent cannot argue with. Identity is not authority: knowing who an agent is tells you nothing about what it may do. Our standard closes that gap with three signatures and nine questions.

  • One approval per model. The authority approves an AI model once and sets the ceilings every mandate on it must respect. It never reviews a customer's limits, and it never sees a customer's data.
  • One signature per customer. The customer writes the mandate: whom the agent may pay, how much, until when. The passport is issued the moment it is signed, within the ceilings, live at once.
  • Nine questions per payment. The bank checks the authority's signature, the registry, the agent's key, the mandate, the payee, the limits, and the supervisor's condition, in order, deny by default, with a signed receipt.
  • Every decision replayable. Refusals reach the authority. Two on the same rule in a day raise an alert. Suspension or revocation takes effect on the next instruction, on every rail.

Our proposal

A payments authority keeps a register of approved AI models, the way it keeps a register of licensed firms. A business puts an agent on an approved model and signs, once, exactly what that agent may pay. A bank checks every instruction against both before money moves, and can prove afterwards why it said yes or no. No new licence for every agent. No review of anyone's invoices. One approval, one signature, nine questions, and a receipt.
