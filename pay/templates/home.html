The numbers

Payment fraud is already an industry. £1.28 billion was taken from UK customers last year, and every pound of it was authorised by someone who believed what they were shown. An agent believes what it is shown too. Give it the right document and it will pay the wrong account with perfect confidence, at machine speed, without a second look. As agents take over the invoice desk, that industry gains a workforce that never sleeps. The only defence that scales with it is a mandate the agent cannot argue with.

The standard

Identity is not authority. Knowing who an agent is tells you nothing about what it may do, and the whole history of payments fraud lives in that gap. Our standard closes it with three signatures and nine questions.

  • One approval per model. The authority approves an AI model once and sets the ceilings every mandate on it must respect. It never reviews a customer's limits, and it never sees a customer's data.
  • One signature per customer. The customer writes the mandate: whom the agent may pay, how much, until when. The passport is issued the moment it is signed, within the ceilings, live at once.
  • Nine questions per payment. The bank checks the authority's signature, the registry, the agent's key, the mandate, the payee, the limits, and the supervisor's condition, in order, deny by default, with a signed receipt.
  • Every decision replayable. Refusals reach the authority. Two on the same rule in a day raise an alert. Suspension or revocation takes effect on the next instruction, on every rail.
