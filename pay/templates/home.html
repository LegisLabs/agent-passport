The shift

Agents are already buying. Last holiday season AI and agents stood behind a fifth of everything sold online, and the businesses behind those shoppers are handing over their invoices next. The question is no longer whether agents will pay, but whether anyone can see on whose authority.

Agentic commerce, global, $ billion: actual to 2025, forecast to 2030

2024 and 2025: online holiday sales influenced by AI and agents, Salesforce 2024, Salesforce 2025. 2030: agentic commerce spend, Juniper Research forecast; McKinsey puts the same year at $3 to 5 trillion, and Juniper expects 1.3 billion people buying through agents by 2031.

The numbers

This is what happens when the person paying cannot see who is really behind a request. Of the £1.28 billion taken from UK customers last year, £576 million was authorised push payment fraud, up 19% in a year: payments the victims made themselves, in good faith, to someone they could not see. An agent sees even less than they did.

The problem

Today an agent's payment looks exactly like a person's. Nothing on it says which agent acted, what it was allowed to do, or who stands behind it. The bank sees an instruction and nothing else.

Identity alone does not fix this. Knowing who an agent is tells you nothing about what it may do, and nothing about who answers when it goes wrong.

Our proposal

We think the answer is not to watch agents more closely, but to make every agent transparent by design: an authority's approval, a customer's signature and a bank's verdict, carried on every payment and provable afterwards. For the first time ever, an AI model becomes an official actor with a defined, provable, revocable authority.

  1. The provider registers a model. Once.
  2. The authority approves. Once.
  3. The customer signs the mandate. Once.
  4. The bank checks. Every payment.
