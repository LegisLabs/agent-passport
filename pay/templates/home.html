Identity is not authority. Delegation can only narrow authority, never expand it.

How it works

  1. A provider registers a model

    Once, with pinned version and attested documentation. No agent, no key, no customer data. Provider Panel

  2. The authority approves it with ceilings

    Automated checks and a six-step Standards Review Assistant inform a case officer. Approval sets the limit per payment, per account, the expiry and a condition. Regulator Panel

  3. A customer registers an agent and signs its mandate

    Which suppliers it may pay, how much, until when, within the ceilings. The passport is issued at signing and is live at once. Customer Panel

  4. The bank verifies every instruction

    Nine checks, deny by default, a signed receipt for each verdict. A manipulated invoice is refused before payment. Action Terminal

  5. Refusals feed back to the authority

    Two refusals on the same rule in a day raise an alert. The authority can suspend, investigate, reinstate or revoke; the rail follows. Audit

Built on open standards

Three signatures in one envelope: the authority's assurance, the customer's agent identity and its payment mandate. The agent signs each instruction with its own key.

  • OAuth 2.0 RFC 9396
  • W3C VC JWT
  • RFC 7800 cnf
  • Ed25519 RFC 8037
  • vouch.finance AI Vouchers

  • BusinessesGive an agent a mandate you wrote and signed. It can never pay outside it.
