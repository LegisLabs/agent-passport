Agent Passport

Identity is not authority. Delegation can only narrow authority, never expand it. Two components, one engine: the Standards Review Assistant (the brief's KY-A Standards Sandbox Copilot) at assurance time, and the Cryptographic Delegation Verifier (R.1–R.9) at payment time. The three-signature envelope realises the Passport / Principal Mandate / Action Intent Manifest model: Layer A agent assurance is the authority signature, Layer B the customer's mandate, Layer C the instruction signed by the agent key, Layer D the bank's R.1–R.9 decision, Layer E the anomaly and escalation feed to the regulator.

What it is

Agent Passport focuses on bridging the delegation gap. It enables a payment institution's AI agent to carry a verifiable, human-approved payment mandate, and enables the relying bank to verify every payment action against that mandate before execution. Agent identity, regulatory assurance, action-time enforcement, anomaly detection, escalation and revocation are supporting components required to make that delegation control credible.

What it is not

This prototype does not attempt to solve: biometric synthetic-identity detection; agent swarm detection at infrastructure scale; complete cross-border mutual recognition; legal liability calculation or consumer-redress compensation; or systemic payment-infrastructure risk modelling. The mandate enforces money destination and authority — not invoice semantic accuracy; full invoice verification is a production extension. These are acknowledged problems in the broader KYA landscape, noted as production roadmap items.

We do not claim: that any supervisor issues agent passports today; that the passport replaces the provider's licence, the customer's contract or the bank's own mandate; that a signature proves an agent is safe or correct; that a valid passport compels a bank to execute; that this is production cryptographic infrastructure. The registers, firms, bank, accounts and invoices are synthetic. The agent and the customer's signing ceremony are simulated inside this server.

Why it matters now

Banks today cannot distinguish a payment instruction submitted by a human treasury manager from one submitted by an autonomous AI agent acting on their behalf. Agent Passport is the proposed framework that closes this gap — cryptographically, deterministically, sub-10ms per payment.

For large B2B banks, the primary drivers for adoption are AML compliance (the Payment Services Regulations 2017 require controls over who submits payment instructions, regardless of whether the payer is a consumer or a corporation) and market access gatekeeping (a bank that certifies which AI agents can use its rails controls a commercial gate in the emerging agent-payment ecosystem). The 2024 APP fraud mandatory reimbursement reform is the consumer and SME incentive story; for large B2B, AML compliance and market access are the accurate framing.

Important distinction: the bank runs R.1–R.9 on every payment instruction the AI submits — deny-by-default, no AI inference, no human in the loop at execution time. A planned Grounds Declaration layer (J.1–J.8) will additionally escalate source-integrity concerns to a named human. The regulator acts on patterns and anomalies, not on individual payments.

The FATF 2025 guidance on AI-enabled circumvention of AML/CFT identity controls makes this framework timely. The mandate's named-beneficiary field and amount ceiling are direct AML controls that prevent the AI from redirecting money, even when the AI itself has been manipulated (R.6 and R.7 in this build). JPMorgan Chase already adds friction to detect intent signals on payments; Agent Passport makes that determination cryptographically provable, not behavioural.

Technical standards

Customer mandate: OAuth 2.0 Rich Authorization Request (RFC 9396), directly implementing NIST NCCoE 2026 guidance on OAuth 2.0 for agentic systems. Credential format: W3C Verifiable Credentials JWT profile (RFC 7519). Agent key binding: RFC 7800 cnf (equivalent workload-identity guarantee to SPIFFE SVID; SPIFFE extension is a production roadmap item). Signatures: Ed25519 / EdDSA (RFC 8037). Bank-side verification: deny-by-default Zero Trust architecture (NIST NCCoE 2026), deterministic, no AI inference at execution time. Ed25519 keys are compatible with W3C did:key DID resolution; full DID document support is a production extension. Revocation anchored via vouch.finance AI Voucher standard. Agent Passport addresses the same delegation-gap problem as Visa TAP and ERC-8004, within the regulated payment institution framework.

Every payment instruction is signed by the agent's key over the canonical JSON of passport_id, action_type, payee_account_ref, supplier_name, amount, currency, invoice_ref, nonce; R.4 verifies the signature bytes against the key in agent_identity.cnf. Flipping one byte fails it.

Regulatory context

The regulator tab demonstrates what an Agent Assurance function would look like within the Payment Services Regulations 2017 framework. This is a design prototype of a proposed extension to an existing authorisation process — not a claimed regulatory approval. The assurance function is AI-assisted but human-decided at every consequential point. The Standards Review Assistant is the brief's "KY-A Standards Sandbox Copilot" brought to life.

Three phases

A model becomes an agent when a customer gives it a mandate. Phase 1: the model company registers its model (company, pinned version, published benchmark data, training details, documentation); a named person attests the documentation is accurate and the company carries no liability for what any agent later does. Phase 2: the authority approves the model once, sets the policy ceilings and the supervisor condition, and keeps two registries, approved models and live passports; it never sees a mandate or a transaction. Phase 3: the customer registers its agent on an approved model (own key, proof of possession, configuration hash, key custody) and its authorising officer writes and signs the mandate within the ceilings; that signature issues the passport and mints the voucher; live at once, no review. The bank enforces all of it on every payment.

Who answers for what

The model company answers only for the accuracy of its registered documentation. The customer answers for the mandate it signed and its deployment of the agent. The bank answers if it executes without checking. The authority holds the registries that make any failure reconstructable. Nobody loses except the attacker.

The vouch rail

Mandate anchored as AI Voucher on vouch.finance — demonstrating interoperability with the open agent-authorisation ecosystem. When the customer signs its mandate the passport is issued and mirrored as an AI Voucher with the customer's limits; on revocation it is deleted, so one supervisory action makes two rails refuse. Allowed instructions are also settled on the rail under a mirror program. This deployment runs vouch in {{ vouch_mode }} mode on the {{ payment_rail }} rail.

Where the model sits, and where it does not

Registration is a form the model company fills in by hand. Gemini is used at the edges only: the agent reads invoices into payment instructions with a verbatim quote per fact, and the officer can ask for a draft file note. It never checks, scores, approves or verifies. If the API is unavailable the system falls back to a deterministic fixture and says so. The consequential middle is the rule pack {{ pack.id }}, so the same inputs always give the same decision.
